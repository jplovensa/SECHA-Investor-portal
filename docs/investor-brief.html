SECHAFIXED-MARGIN PACKAGE ECONOMICS

US$10,000 minimum.
30% gross margin. Always.

Every package is priced at a fixed 30% gross margin. The fulfilment budget is 70% of package value. Larger packages preserve the same percentage; contribution after acquisition and support is a separate metric.

Entry-package pricing rule US$
Minimum package value 10,000
Fulfilment budget / 70% − 7,000
Package gross profit / 30% 3,000

The fulfilment ledger needs to cover materials/components, factory preparation, installation, freight, quality and rework within the budget. No invented category-level actual cost schedule is presented.

Contribution sensitivity / illustrative defaults

Assumed acquisition cost / package US$200
Assumed incremental sales/support / package US$100
First-package contribution / 27% US$2,700
Assumed fixed operating expense / month US$20,000
Operating break-even 8 jobs / month

Cash timing

At a 50% deposit, US$5,000 is collected against US$7,000 fulfilment: a US$2,000 pre-final-payment delivery gap per package, or US$20,000 at ten concurrent jobs.

Higher-value example

A US$20,000 package has a US$14,000 fulfilment budget and US$6,000 gross profit. Gross margin remains 30%; at the same US$300 acquisition/support assumption, contribution is US$5,700.

CAC, support, overhead and deposit figures are assumptions, not operating actuals. Contribution = 30% × selected package value − CAC − incremental support. Break-even = ceiling(fixed overhead ÷ positive contribution). Delivery cash gap assumes all fulfilment is paid before final collection and no supplier credit; taxes, financing and other unprovided costs are excluded.
