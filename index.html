05 / THE MATERIAL & ASSEMBLY SYSTEMMORE THAN A SNAP PANEL. A RENOVATION KIT.

Develop the material.
Pack the renovation.
Assemble the space.

Snap panel is the starting component, not the whole product. SECHA’s development scope is broader: unconventional composite materials, repeatable components, flatpack packaging and a site assembly sequence that turns a renovation into a kit.

01 / WALL SYSTEM

Snap panel / EPS-core walls.

Expanded-polystyrene cores paired with rigid facings, finished surfaces and repeatable connection details. The development goal is a wall system that ships as flat components and assembles in a defined sequence.

02 / FURNITURE SYSTEM

EPS-core tables.

Composite tabletops use EPS as an internal core, with engineered face sheets, reinforced edges and a supporting frame. EPS fills the panel; the structure and connections carry the intended loads.

03 / SPACE SYSTEM

EPS-core partitions.

Framed, finished composite panels define rooms and zones. Modular panel sizes, rigid bases and replaceable connection details belong to the same flatpack assembly language.

01 Specify the space02 Engineer the components03 Pack flat04 Assemble on site

The interface is the front end.
The material system makes the promise deliverable.

A job file should become a bill of materials, a component set, a packaging plan and an assembly sequence. That is how SECHA aims to replace repeated bespoke work with a product the business can deliver again.

PROPOSED PRODUCT ROADMAP / RELEASE GATES, NOT DATES

From a material experiment
to a renovation you can order.

The unit of innovation is the complete room kit: compatible parts, a defined scope, a packing list and an assembly method. Each release earns the next.

  1. 01 / MATERIAL PLATFORM

    Prove the composite.

    Prototype Snap panel skins, EPS cores, edges and connections. Extend the material platform to supported tabletops and framed partitions.

    RELEASE GATE Engineer-reviewed specifications and applicable load, fire, moisture and durability tests before product release.

  2. 02 / COMPONENT LIBRARY

    Make the parts compatible.

    Define panel modules, table frames, partition bases and connectors. Version each SKU, finish, tolerance, supplier and replacement part.

    RELEASE GATE Repeatable fit, costed bills of materials and documented assembly trials.

  3. 03 / PILOT ROOM KIT

    Ship one complete renovation.

    Bundle a constrained room scope into labelled flatpacks with a packing list, tools, site requirements and an installer sequence. Pilot in Greater Jakarta.

    RELEASE GATE Validate transport damage, missing parts, installation time, snagging and actual fulfilment cost against the 70% budget.

  4. 04 / CONFIGURABLE CATALOGUE

    Turn play into a package.

    Connect approved SECHA+ choices to compatible kits, a controlled job file and an order-ready scope. Expand the catalogue only when delivery is repeatable.

    RELEASE GATE Reconcile concept, component list, price and installed result; keep the US$10,000 minimum and 30% gross-margin pricing rule.

THE PRODUCT: a configured space + a defined component kit + flatpack delivery + a repeatable installation. Proposed development stages; no completion dates or validation results are claimed.

A CLOSER LOOK

The investor essentials.

What is SECHA’s core business?

SECHA earns on renovation delivery. Its interface turns customer choices into a controlled package and job file. Material development, flatpack packaging and modular assembly make the physical delivery repeatable. Financing helps customers afford a package and supports conversion.

What has already been demonstrated?

The business update reports US$300K in project value served and 40% faster modular delivery. The next step is to productise founder-led delivery into measurable package throughput. These figures are company-reported and are not independently verified here.

How is the 30% package margin set?

SECHA’s current pricing rule is a minimum US$10,000 package with a fixed 30% gross margin. At entry price, the fulfilment budget is US$7,000 and package gross profit is US$3,000. The model keeps that percentage fixed for larger packages; contribution after acquisition and support is a separate metric. This pricing policy is not a restatement of historical realised margins.

Is the US$100M scale case a forecast?

No. It is an updated illustration of 10,000 annual packages at the US$10,000 minimum each, with US$30M in gross profit at the fixed 30% margin. It is not current revenue, a forecast or a committed pipeline.
