01 / THE MANIFESTOPEOPLE MAKE SPACE.
OUR POINT OF VIEW

The world doesn’t
just need more space.
It needs better
ways to make it.

A conviction behind SECHA.
Josephine LovensaFOUNDER & CEO

A home is lived in. A space is made personal. The process of making it should feel just as considered.

Today, renovation asks customers to buy uncertainty: an evolving brief, a moving price and a finish line that keeps changing. Every new project asks the business to start again.

We believe the next renovation business will make choice simpler and delivery more disciplined. Not by making every space the same, but by making the system behind each space repeatable.

I.

Freedom in the choices.

A customer should be able to see, explore and shape a space before committing to it.

II.

Clarity in the commitment.

Scope, materials, price and sequence belong in one controlled job file.

III.

Discipline in the delivery.

The physical system should fulfil what the interface promises.

A CLOSER LOOK

The investor essentials.

What is SECHA’s core business?

SECHA earns on renovation delivery. Its interface turns customer choices into a controlled package and job file; GX-100 standardises physical fulfilment. Financing helps customers afford a package and supports conversion.

What has already been demonstrated?

The business update reports US$300K in project value served and 40% faster modular delivery. The next step is to productise founder-led delivery into measurable package throughput. These figures are company-reported and are not independently verified here.

Is the 30% gross margin already achieved?

No realised package margin is stated in the business update. The 30% figure is the target package gross margin: US$1,500 gross profit on an illustrative US$5,000 package.

Is the US$50M scale case a forecast?

No. It is an illustration of 10,000 annual packages at US$5,000 each, with US$15M in gross profit if the 30% target margin is achieved. It is not current revenue, a forecast or a committed pipeline.
